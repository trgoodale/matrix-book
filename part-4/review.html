Part IV review

These exercises mix ideas from Parts I to IV. They don’t say which chapter they come from, so part of each exercise is deciding which tools to use. Solutions, with the chapters each exercise draws on, are in Solutions: Part IV.

  1. Find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of \(\M{A} = \mat{2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2}\). Use them to find \(\M{A}^{10}\) exactly.

    Solution to exercise 1

  2. Each year, 10% of people in a town move to the suburbs and 20% of people in the suburbs move to the town; nobody leaves or arrives. Write down the transition matrix, find its eigenvalues, and find the long-run split between town and suburbs. Roughly how quickly does the population approach this split?

    Solution to exercise 2

  3. Sketch the curve \(5x^2 + 4xy + 2y^2 = 6\). Find the directions of its axes and their half-lengths. Is it an ellipse or a hyperbola, and how can you tell from the matrix alone?

    Solution to exercise 3

  4. Find the singular values of \(\M{A} = \mat{3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5}\), by finding the eigenvalues of \(\M{A}\T\M{A}\). Check that their product is \(\lvert\det\M{A}\rvert\), and find the condition number \(\kappa_2(\M{A})\). How far (in the 2-norm) is \(\M{A}\) from the nearest singular matrix?

    Solution to exercise 4

  5. Solve \[\frac{d\vect{x}}{dt} = \mat{-2 & 1 \\ 1 & -2}\vect{x}, \qquad \vect{x}(0) = \mat{1 \\ 0}.\] Which part of the solution dies away fastest, and what does each part mean physically if \(x_1\) and \(x_2\) are the temperatures of two connected objects?

    Solution to exercise 5

  6. Let \(\M{A} = \mat{2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2}\) and \(\vect{b} = (1, 1)\T\). Find the minimum of \(f(\vect{x}) = \vect{x}\T\M{A}\vect{x} - 2\vect{b}\T\vect{x}\) by setting the gradient to zero, and explain how you know it is a minimum and not a maximum or saddle.

    Solution to exercise 6

  7. Find the eigenvalues of \(\M{B} = \mat{1 & -2 \\ 1 & 1}\). What happens to the vectors \(\vect{x}_k = \M{B}^k\vect{x}_0\) as \(k\) grows? What happens to the solutions of \(d\vect{x}/dt = \M{B}\vect{x}\)?

    Solution to exercise 7

  8. A transformation of the plane stretches the direction \((2, 1)\T\) by a factor of 4 and leaves every vector along \((1, 1)\T\) unchanged. Find its matrix \(\M{A}\). Then find \(\det\M{A}\) and \(\M{A}^{-1}\), and say what \(\M{A}^{-1}\) does to each of the two directions.

    Solution to exercise 8

  9. Let \(\M{B} = \mat{2 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 2}\). Find the rank of \(\M{B} - \M{I}\), and use it to show that 1 is an eigenvalue of \(\M{B}\) and to find the dimension of its eigenspace. Find a basis for that eigenspace, and the remaining eigenvalue and its eigenvector. Check that eigenvectors for different eigenvalues are perpendicular. Why was this bound to happen?

    Solution to exercise 9

  10. The reflection in the line \(y = 2x\) has matrix \(\M{R} = \tfrac{1}{5}\mat{-3 & 4 \\ 4 & 3}\). Without solving the characteristic equation, write down its eigenvalues and eigenvectors, using what a reflection does to vectors along the mirror and across it. Check your answer with the trace and the determinant. What are \(\M{R}^2\) and \(\M{R}^{25}\)?

    Solution to exercise 10

  11. Code. For the system in exercise 5, compute \(\vect{x}(1)\) in two ways: with the built-in matrix exponential, and with \(\M{V}e^{\M{\Lambda}t}\M{V}^{-1}\vect{x}(0)\) using the eigenvalues and eigenvectors. Compare both with your formula.

    Solution to exercise 11
